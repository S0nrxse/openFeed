# Signals and one-user modelling: literature review for OpenFeed
Setting: 1-2 users, under 20 watches, 81 subscriptions, 80k candidates, no population, no labels, pure Python. Read in full text (pypdf extraction): Covington, D2Q, DVR, D2Co, Hu/Koren/Volinsky, PinnerSage, MIND, ComiRec, Ding&Li, Koren 2009, BellKor 2009, DFN, Seznam. Abstract or secondary only: Yi 2014, Zhao 2018 (DEERS), Rocchio/IIR, Liu/Wu/Resnick. Not read: Rashid 2002 (PDF unreachable). Raw text is in research/pdf/*/p.txt. Correction: "Xie 2021" DFN is IJCAI 2020. "WLR, Zheng 2022" is DVR (Zheng); WLR itself is Covington 2016. I could not identify a paper "Wu et al., Beyond clicks: dwell time"; the real title is Yi et al. 2014.

## 1. Watch time as a label

**F1. Weighted logistic regression for expected watch time.** Covington, Adams, Sargin, RecSys 2016. https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/45530.pdf Clicked impressions are weighted by watch time T, unclicked get weight 1; learned odds approximate E[T](1+P). Reason given: CTR ranking "often promotes deceptive videos that the user does not complete (clickbait)". Result: equal weighting raised the watch-time-weighted loss by 4.1% (relative to the 1024-512-256 model, 41.6% base class). Internal YouTube data, no CIs. Transfer: the idea (rank by expected watch, not click) transfers; the estimator does not. OpenFeed has no impressions (feed_impressions has 0 rows) so there are no unclicked negatives, and the model needs millions of rows. Take-away: the label should be watch fraction or watch seconds, never "opened".

**F2. Duration bias is real and fixing it moves metrics.** D2Q, Zhan et al., KDD 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06003 Kuaishou, full-screen feed. Split by duration into M groups, regress the within-group watch-time quantile. Offline XAUC: value regression 0.6843, WLR 0.6940, D2Q 0.7084, Res-D2Q (30 groups) 0.7150. Live 24h A/B: WLR +0.184% watch time, Res-D2Q +0.746% (authors: typical production gain about 0.1%). Note WLR MAE (65.7) was worse than plain regression (28.2). Peak at 30 groups; more groups degrade from small group samples. Transfer: the principle transfers (normalise by duration); 30 groups do not. With 12 playback rows, any per-duration-bin quantile is noise. A fixed parametric correction is the only feasible form, for example label = watched_seconds / min(duration, c) or a log-duration reference curve. Short-video feed bias differs from YouTube-style long video where the user picks the video.

**F3. Watch-time gain, DVR.** Zheng et al., MM 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05190 Shows common metrics (watch time, even AUC) favour long videos. WTG = watch time relative to the mean of videos in the same duration level. WeChat/Kuaishou datasets: WTG@10 for FM 0.0209 -> 0.1332 with DVR (adversarial debiasing, needs training). Transfer: WTG as an evaluation metric transfers conceptually. Needs a population average per duration bin, which we lack; the 79k-video corpus with view counts cannot supply that (view counts are not watch ratios).

**F4. Watch ratio is not interest when the video is fully played.** D2Co/CWM, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07932 Argues completion (ratio = 1) is truncation of the true watch time and users differ in explicit feedback among completed videos. Online A/B, 5% traffic arms, tens of millions of users: mean watch time +2.9%, valid views +2.5%, CTR +0.3%. Offline on three datasets. Transfer: a qualitative point only. For a household, a 100% ratio on a 3 minute clip is weak evidence and on a 40 minute video is strong evidence. Absolute seconds alone over-credits long videos; ratio alone over-credits short ones. Use both (for example min(ratio, 1) * log(1+seconds)).

**F5. Watch time as sole satisfaction proxy hurts.** Florian et al. (Seznam), ORSUM@RecSys 2020, https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2715/paper7.pdf Optimising watch time alone "leads to a vast harm on CTR"; they combine models online. No effect sizes were read beyond that. Yi et al., RecSys 2014 (https://bibtex.github.io/RecSys-2014-YiHZLR.html): dwell time as a per-item relevance proxy, normalised across devices. I read only the abstract (via secondary summaries), no numbers. Evidence for "dwell beats click" is therefore asserted by authors, not verified here. The cheap, defensible reading: treat very short playback (under about 10% or 30 s) as weak negative, not neutral. No dataset-backed threshold was verified; do not cite one.

## 2. Implicit confidence, explicit feedback

**F6. Hu, Koren, Volinsky, ICDM 2008.** http://yifanhu.net/PUB/cf.pdf Implicit data gives preference p=1 if observed, with confidence c = 1 + alpha*r (r = amount of consumption); alpha=40 "found to produce good results". Unobserved items are weak negatives, not unknown. Evaluated on a TV viewing dataset with expected percentile rank versus popularity and neighbourhood baselines. Transfer: the confidence form transfers as a weighting rule (strong evidence = high weight, absence = near zero), the factor model does not. alpha=40 is tuned for a count scale and cannot be reused for seconds. DFN (Xie et al. IJCAI 2020, https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2020/349): dislike is "precise but rare" (0.15% of feedback, 0.65M of 448M events); best loss weight on dislike was 10x a click (tested 5, 10, 50, 100). Online: dislike rate -33.17%, CTR +1.17%. Transfer: the ratio "one explicit negative outweighs about 10 weak signals" is the only portable number; the model is not.

## 3. Negative feedback

**F7. Rocchio.** Manning, Raghavan, Schutze, IIR ch. 9 (secondary summaries only; site unreachable from here). q' = a*q + b*mean(relevant) - g*mean(non-relevant). Suggested a=1, b=0.75, g=0.15 (some slides 0.25); g < b because positive documents are more informative; negative term weights clipped to zero; many systems use g=0. Transfer: directly implementable on TF-IDF with 80k vectors. Weak evidence: the values are conventions from IR test collections, not tuned on video feeds. The clipping is important: subtracting a vector from a sparse TF-IDF profile otherwise creates nonsense negative terms.

**F8. Skips as negatives.** Zhao et al., KDD 2018, https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06501 (DEERS). JD.com e-commerce, evaluated with an offline simulator, not live users. Skipped items outnumber clicks, so positives get buried; fix is architectural (DQN). Transfer: none, other than a caution that implicit negatives must not be pooled with clicks at equal weight.

**F9. What do "not interested" and "don't recommend channel" do?** Liu, Wu, Resnick, ICWSM 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14551 Sock-puppet agents on live YouTube: "Not interested" cut the target topic on the homepage by 88% on average; neither stain nor scrub had much effect on video-page (watch-next) recommendations. Survey N=300: 44% unaware of the button. Mozilla 2022 (press summaries only, not read): "Not interested" about 11% and "Don't recommend channel" about 43% of unwanted videos prevented. Contradictory: 88% vs 11% for the same button on different methodology. Treat both as "black-box effects measured from the outside", not design guidance. What they do show is that a hide affects the topic and surface, not one video. How should a hide generalise? No paper gives a calibrated rule. Defensible design from F7/F9/F10: (a) the exact video, hard exclude; (b) near neighbours, a Rocchio-style subtraction of its topic vector with weight g about 0.15-0.25 of a positive, clipped at zero; (c) the channel, a Bayesian per-channel penalty that grows with the count of independent negatives on that channel and is not applied after one hide (see F10); (d) an explicit "mute channel" action as the only hard channel block.

## 4. Cold start, priors, shrinkage

**F10. Shrinkage with pseudo-counts.** Koren/BellKor Netflix Prize solution, https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/statistics/statgen/joul_aut2009/BellKor.pdf b_i = sum(r - mu) / (lambda1 + n_i), lambda1 = 25 (item), lambda2 = 10 (user), chosen by validation on 100M-rating Netflix data. So an item needed about 25 ratings before its own mean carried equal weight to the prior. Transfer: the form transfers exactly (it is just a Beta/normal prior). The numbers are scale-specific to ratings on a 5-star scale. For OpenFeed set lambda by what a wrong channel penalty costs, not by Netflix. No published threshold exists for "how many examples before personal beats prior" for this setting. I looked for Rashid et al. 2002 and could not read it. Any number I would give is my own, flagged as a hypothesis (H4, H7), not a finding.

**F11. Subscriptions as seeds.** I found no paper that tests subscription lists as profile seeds against a prior. The content-based cold start literature (Rashid 2002; Schein 2002) was not retrieved. Weak evidence, based on reasoning only: a subscription is a deliberate opt-in with higher precision than a watch, but it is a statement about a channel in the past, not about today's interests. Treat it as a prior of fixed pseudo-count weight that watches then override, not as watches.

## 5. Drift and decay

**F12. Published half-lives disagree.**
- Ding and Li, CIKM 2005, https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/classes/fa17/cse291-b/reading/p485-ding.pdf: they say weight halves in T0 days but their formula is exp(-t/T0) (an inconsistency in the paper); T0 in {10, 20, 50, 100, 200} on EachMovie ratings; the best T0 differs by training size and column (MAE 0.13 to 0.21), and a per-item-cluster personalised "auto" decay beat or matched fixed values in most columns. Sparse-vs-dense effect: MAE spread across T0 is tiny (0.2056-0.2063) in one column and large (0.1300-0.1723) in another.
- Pinterest, KDD 2020: decay weight exp(-lambda*age), lambda=0.01 "a good balance", 0.1 weighted toward recent, 0 toward frequent. The unit is not stated in the text I read; if days, half-life is about 69 days (lambda 0.01) or 6.9 days (0.1). Offline, tens of millions of users: decay average lifts accuracy +25% (cosine > 0.8 next-action task) over last-pin; ranking lift 8% R-Prec for lambda=0.01 variant.
- Koren, KDD 2009, https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~zha/CSE8801/CF/kdd-fp074-koren.pdf: "consistent finding was that prediction quality improves as we moderate that time decay, reaching best quality when there is no decay at all", because decay discards signal when data per user is scarce. Transfer: for under 20 watches Koren's finding is the relevant one: with so few events, decaying them throws away most of the information. A mild decay (half-life of months) is justified only as a tie-breaker. Fast session-level interest is not tested in any of these papers on video. Contradiction is genuine (Ding&Li vs Koren), and nothing here is video-specific.

## 6. Multi-interest

**F13. MIND.** Li et al., CIKM 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08030. Amazon Books HR@50: one interest 0.0978, K=3 interests 0.1101 (+12.6% relative); YouTube DNN 0.0746. Tmall live CTR improved over item-CF and YouTube DNN (figure only).

**F14. ComiRec.** Cen et al., KDD 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09347. Amazon/Taobao: multi-interest beats MIND and GRU4Rec on recall/NDCG; best K varies (2 to 8) by model and dataset, so K is not universal. Has a diversity term sum f(u,i) + lambda * sum g(i,j) with category dissimilarity. Transfer: neither works at our scale (hundreds of thousands to millions of users and sequences of dozens). The finding that transfers is that K=1 loses, about 10% relative, and K needs to be small (2 to 4).

**F15. PinnerSage.** Pal et al., KDD 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03634. Cluster the last 90 days of actions with Ward linkage (automatic cluster count; light users 3-5 clusters, heavy 75-100), represent each cluster by its medoid (an item the user touched, robust to outliers), sample 3 medoids by importance sum(exp(-lambda*age)). Offline ranking lift over last-pin: decay average 8%, sample one embedding 24%, k-means k=5 32%, complete linkage 29%, Ward+medoid 37%. Online: Homefeed +4% volume, +2% propensity; Shopping +20% / +8%. Gain in relevance tapers beyond 3 retrieved embeddings. Transfer: the best one for us because it needs no training, only a similarity. Works on any embedding, including TF-IDF cosine or YouTube related-graph similarity. With under 20 watches Ward on 20 points is trivial and cheap, and medoids are inspectable (PRODUCT.md). Weakness: Pinterest's input is a learned PinSage embedding, our TF-IDF is much noisier, ASCII-only, and cosine on sparse short titles is low-signal; the gains may not carry over. Max-sim over exemplars (score = max over exemplars of cos(candidate, exemplar), weighted by exemplar importance) is the simplest form of the same idea.

## Summary of transfer
Transfers: duration-normalised watch label; confidence weighting with explicit negatives much stronger than implicit; pseudo-count shrinkage; Rocchio with clipped negatives; medoid/exemplar multi-interest with K small; weak or no decay at low counts. Does not transfer: WLR/D2Q group quantiles, DVR, CWM, DFN, DEERS, MIND, ComiRec (all need training sets of millions). Missing evidence: video-specific half-lives, subscription seeding, a calibrated hide-to-channel rule, any rule for when personal beats prior. The honest status of those four is "design choice to be tested on our own data".

## Falsifiable hypotheses for OpenFeed
Each needs an offline replay on the real user's events (not the 'local' user). With roughly 12 watches, leave-one-out is the only protocol and CIs will be wide; effect sizes below are plausible, not predicted by the literature.
H1. Change: label = min(ratio,1) * log(1+seconds) instead of any opened/started flag. Effect: users' replay rank of held-out watched videos improves vs binary label. Falsify: leave-one-out median rank of the held-out watch does not improve in paired bootstrap (95% CI includes 0) with n=12. Cost: low (one function).
H2. Change: treat playback under 10% of duration as a weak negative (weight about -0.2 of a full positive). Effect: fewer re-recommendations of videos the user bounced from. Falsify: bounced-video re-impression rate in a 2-week replay/prod log is unchanged, or the leave-one-out rank gets worse. Cost: low.
H3. Change: Rocchio profile q = subs_prior + b*watches - g*hide/not-interested, g/b about 0.2, clipped at zero. Effect: share of top-50 whose cosine to any hidden video exceeds 0.5 falls by half vs hide-only-removes-one. Falsify: that share does not drop, or held-out positive rank drops more than the CI of H1. Cost: medium (profile builder + clip).
H4. Change: seed profile from the 81 subscriptions with pseudo-count k (try 10), and let each watch count as 1; shrink toward the prior as sum = (k*prior + sum w_i x_i)/(k+n). Effect: with n<20, held-out watched videos rank higher than with the empty-profile recency feed. Falsify: no gain in leave-one-out rank at n=5, 10, 15, or gains vanish when k varies 5 to 20 (then the prior is carrying nothing). Cost: low-medium.
H5. Change: channel penalty = log(1+negatives)/(lambda+impressions-or-videos-seen), no effect after a single hide. Effect: a channel with 3+ hides drops out of the top 100 while one-hide channels are unaffected. Falsify: the user re-hides videos from channels with 1 hide as often as before (no false-positive cost), or valid watches from penalised channels exceed 10% of held-out watches. Cost: medium.
H6. Change: 2-4 exemplar profile (Ward clusters over watches + liked, medoids), score = max-sim weighted by exemplar importance, instead of one mean vector. Effect: on held-out watches the rank improves versus single centroid (PinnerSage reported +37% vs +8% but on learned embeddings). Falsify: no improvement in leave-one-out at n>=10, or the held-out video's nearest exemplar similarity is not higher than similarity to the centroid. Cost: medium.
H7. Change: crossover rule, personal weight w = n/(n+k) with k=10 (shrinks away from the prior as watches accumulate). Effect: personal beats prior by n about 10. Falsify: leave-one-out rank of personal-only profile is not better than prior-only at n=10 across at least 20 resampled orderings of the 12 watches. Cost: low, but only informative with resampling.
H8. Change: decay half-life 90 days on watches versus none (Koren) versus 14 days (Pinterest lambda 0.1). Effect: no difference below 20 watches; 90 d neutral or slightly positive. Falsify: either decay variant changes leave-one-out rank by more than the noise floor of H1 in the same direction at 12 watches. Cost: low.
H9. Change: duration-normalised score for ranking shelves (down-weight raw duration so a 3 hour video does not win on watch seconds). Effect: median duration of top-50 moves closer to median duration of watched videos. Falsify: duration distribution of top-50 is unchanged, or the user's own watch ratio on recommended items falls. Cost: low.
