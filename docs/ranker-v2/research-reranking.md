# Re-ranking, diversity, freshness, exploration: literature review for OpenFeed

Setting used for every transfer verdict: 1-2 users, under 20 watches, 81 subscriptions, ~80k candidates, no other users, no A/B traffic.
Read status: R = I read the abstract and parts of the full text this session. A = abstract/secondary summary only. N = known from memory, not re-read, treat as unverified.

## 0. Summary of the evidence problem
Every industrial result below comes from millions of users with randomised A/B arms (effects of +0.07% to +3.5%). Nothing here measures a
single household. What transfers is mechanism and design (what to log, how to randomise, what to cap), not effect sizes. Apart from Li 2010
(sparse-data section) no source I read tests the regime of tens of events per week.

## 1. Diversification
- Carbonell and Goldstein 1998, MMR (SIGIR). N. score = lambda*rel - (1-lambda)*max sim(to already chosen). Greedy, O(k*n). Evaluated on document summarisation/retrieval, not feeds. Transfers as a mechanism (it is the cheap version of what _diversify_recommendations does with hard caps), but there is no feed-level evidence in the original.
- Wilhelm et al. 2018, DPP at YouTube. CIKM, https://doi.org/10.1145/3269206.3272018. A (abstract via search summary; full text not retrievable here). Claims: a DPP kernel (quality x similarity), trained from user preference examples, re-ranks homepage candidates; live traffic showed short- and long-term engagement gains. I could not verify magnitudes. Does not transfer: the kernel is learned from large volumes of user-clicked sets. A hand-set DPP (quality = our score, similarity = TF-IDF cosine + same-channel) is just a principled MMR, with log-det replacing max-sim; fast greedy MAP is cheap at k=50.
- Steck 2018, Calibrated Recommendations (RecSys), https://doi.org/10.1145/3240323.3240372. A (search summary; PDF unreachable). Re-rank so the category distribution q of the list matches the distribution p in the user's history, minimising KL(p||q~) with a submodular greedy (1-1/e). Offline MovieLens/Netflix-style experiments, not online. Later critique (https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03239 family, A): KL form can misbehave for the approximation guarantee. Transfers partially: with <20 watches p is mostly noise, so calibrate to a prior mixed from subscriptions (81 channels -> topic mass) rather than to watches; shrink p toward the subscription distribution with weight ~ n/(n+k).
- xQuAD (Santos 2010, N) and slate models (e.g. SlateQ, Ie et al. 2019, N): need learned per-item response models and user populations. Not applicable.
- Sliding-window / channel spacing: no controlled evidence found in what I read. LinkedIn's feed paper summary (A, search result on Agarwal et al. 2015 "Personalizing LinkedIn Feed") says a model with freshness + diversity + connection terms beat reverse chronological on CTR, size not verified. So there is no online evidence that hard caps are worse than MMR/DPP at k~50; none that they are better.
- Comparison with our greedy caps: caps are a degenerate MMR (penalty 0 until the cap, infinite after). Weakness is the cliff: item 3 from a channel is as good as item 1 until it is forbidden. A smooth penalty (rank-based multiplicative decay per channel/topic) keeps the cap semantics and removes the cliff; no published evidence it matters for us, so test it (H3).
- Evidence quality: diversity gains in industry are mostly reported as engagement/CTR, not satisfaction. With one user, "diversity" is only defensible as a stated product value (PRODUCT.md), not as a metric you can show wins.

## 2. Serendipity, feedback loops, what to optimise
- Chaney et al. 2018, https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11214. R (abstract). Simulation only: training on data already shaped by the recommender homogenises user behaviour without raising utility. Transfers directly as a warning: our own impressions will bias any future learning. Argues for logging propensities and some randomisation (section 6).
- Jiang et al. 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10730. R (abstract). Theory plus simulation of degenerate loops; their practical remedy is exploration. Same caveat: simulation.
- Kleinberg, Mullainathan, Raghavan 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11776. R (abstract). Theoretical model: users have inconsistent in-the-moment preferences, engagement-only observation lets the platform serve content users would not endorse. No data. Implication for us: watch time/click is a biased proxy; explicit signals (more_like_this, not_interested, "worth my time" prompts) deserve higher weight than dwell.
- Milli, Pierson, Garg 2023 (Twitter), https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16941. R (abstract plus method text). Preregistered audit, ~ a few thousand US users rating timelines: engagement-ranked political tweets were amplified in anger/out-group hostility, and users did not prefer them over reverse-chronological; ranking on stated preference reduced that but risked reinforcing pro-attitudinal content. Transfers as one result: stated preference and engagement diverge. Does not transfer: social-feed political content, not subscriptions video.
- Stray et al. 2021, https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.10939. R (abstract). Survey/position; no effect sizes. Useful only as a source for "ask users" ideas.
- Zhao et al. 2019, YouTube multitask ranking (RecSys), https://doi.org/10.1145/3298689.3346997. A (secondary summaries; not the tables). Ranker predicts engagement and satisfaction (survey-derived) objectives with MMoE, plus a shallow tower that learns position bias from position/device features. A blog reports +0.24% AUC from the shallow tower, unverified. Transfers: the design idea of a separate bias term for position, and satisfaction as its own label. Does not transfer: deep model needs millions of rows.
- Contradiction to note: engagement is both the best available training label (Zhao, Chen) and a poor welfare proxy (Milli, Kleinberg). No resolution at our scale; the pragmatic line is to rank on engagement but constrain with explicit negatives and a capped exploration share.

## 3. Exploration with very few events
- Chen et al. 2019, top-K REINFORCE at YouTube (WSDM), https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02353. R (full text). Off-policy correction with logged behaviour-policy probabilities; live test with 90/5/5 user split: +0.07% ViewTime (significant) from a stochastic policy seen by only 5% of users; top-K correction beat K=1 by +0.66%. Does not transfer as an algorithm (millions of actions, neural policy). Transfers as an argument for logging the behaviour policy's probability for every shown item.
- Chen et al. 2021, Values of User Exploration (RecSys), https://doi.org/10.1145/3460231.3474236. A (search summary). Offline RL analysis of how exploration changes accuracy/diversity/novelty/serendipity; no numbers verified. Not usable except as a pointer.
- Su et al. 2024, Long-Term Value of Exploration (WSDM), https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07764. R (full text). Short-term A/B on exploration is neutral or negative on engagement; benefits appear in corpus growth: discoverable corpus up roughly +50% to +119% across thresholds, +1.25% unique topics with positive interaction per user, positive-feedback gain about 1.4-1.5% in some windows (with CI). Neural linear bandit on a short-video platform. Transfers: the finding that exploration will look like a cost in short-run metrics. Does not transfer: gains come from a creator ecosystem with new uploads needing first impressions.
- Mahajan et al. 2023, PIE (WWW), https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06844. R (full text). Facebook Watch. Thompson sampling over creators per user (reward = a video engaged from that creator, Beta posterior), blending layer holds exploratory impressions at about 6% of a session. Result: +3.50% strong creator connections, +0.85% novel creator connections, engagement regression -0.53% in one arm, neutral overall. This is the closest design to ours and the only one that works per-user: a Beta-Bernoulli bandit has two numbers per arm and needs no population. Transfers well structurally (arm = channel or topic cluster, slots = about 5-10% of the feed). Effect sizes do not.
- Li et al. 2010, LinUCB (WWW), https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.0146. R (full text). Yahoo front page, 33M events: +12.5% CTR over context-free e-greedy; advantage grows as data shrinks (tested down to 1% of data). Closest evidence on scarce data, but 1% of 33M is still 330k events. LinUCB needs a feature vector per arm (we have TF-IDF) but with <20 watches the ridge prior dominates, so it behaves like prior-driven exploration. Prefer Beta-Bernoulli TS over channels.
- How much: PIE 6%, Chen 2019 5% of users. For one user with perhaps 30 views/day, 10-15% of slots randomised is the plausible range; below that you accumulate fewer than ~10 exploration impressions per day and learn nothing.
- Explaining it: no study I read tests explanation of exploration. PRODUCT.md demands inspectable ranking, so label slots "Trying something new (random pick from X)" and log the label. This is a product choice, not evidence-backed.
- Fact check against the brief's list: Chen 2021 arXiv ID I guessed (2008.01190) is wrong (it is a music paper); do not cite it.

## 4. Freshness
- HN: (points-1)/(hours+2)^1.8. A (community-reported news.arc constants). Reddit hot: sign*log10(max(|score|,1)) + age_seconds/45000 from a 2005 epoch, so a 10x score is worth 12.5 hours. A (third-party gist; constants unverified). Both are hand-set for news-like content with votes and have no published validation.
- I found no controlled study of recency decay for evergreen versus news video. Netflix (Gomez-Uribe and Hunt 2015, R, https://gwern.net/doc/reinforcement-learning/exploration/2015-gomezuribe.pdf) discusses a separate Trending Now row for recency instead of one blended score; that is design guidance, not measured decay.
- Practical rule that follows (my inference, not evidence): decay should depend on the video class. For subscription uploads, age relative to that channel's own cadence (hours/typical gap) is better than absolute age: a weekly channel's 5-day-old upload is fresh. For discovery candidates (79k, mostly old), age should be a weak term; old videos deserve a slot when (a) semantic or graph similarity to a recent positive is high, or (b) it is in a rotated "from the archive" shelf. All parameters would be hand-set; H4 tests them.

## 5. Page composition
- Netflix 2015 (R, link above): since 2015 a page-generation algorithm picks and orders rows by relevance and page diversity from tens of thousands of candidate rows, replacing a rule template. No numbers given for it. Transfers: model shelves as candidates and dedupe across rows explicitly (a video appears once per page, claimed by the first row that wants it).
- Joachims et al. 2005, clickthrough bias (SIGIR). A (abstract and course notes). Eye tracking shows top-down scan and "trust bias"; second result viewed roughly 60%, clicked 15% (secondary, unverified). Transfers qualitatively: clicks are position-confounded and relative preferences (clicked vs skipped-above) are more reliable than absolute ones. Grid layouts have different bias than a list and no source I read measures it.
- Lee et al. 2014, Impression discounting at LinkedIn (KDD). A (slides summary and a patent text). Learns a discount for items shown but ignored. Our version: multiply score by f(number of prior impressions without click) over a rolling window; needs the impressions table (section 6). Transfers; learned f does not, so hand-set f = 0.7^k and test.
- Rotation across visits: follows from impression discounting. No quantified benefit found other than the LinkedIn claim.

## 6. Impression logging so counterfactual evaluation is valid
Zero rows exist in feed_impressions today, so no offline evaluation of any ranker change is possible. Evaluation references: Li 2011 replay (https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.5956, R abstract) and Schnabel 2016 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05352, R abstract): unbiased estimates require logged randomisation or known propensities. Minimum fields per shown item:
 (visit_id, shelf_id/name, position within shelf, shelf rank on page, video_id, channel_id, ranker_version, config hash, per-feature score components, final score, candidate-pool size, whether the item came from an exploration slot, p_show (probability this item was selected: 1.0 for deterministic, known epsilon for random slots), viewport/device, timestamp), plus whether it was seen (scrolled into view) versus merely rendered, and later outcomes joined by visit_id. Also log items scored and not shown near the cut (top 200 with scores) to allow what-if reranking.
Minimal randomised design for one user (my design, standard practice, not from a source):
 1. Per-visit random seed. In 10% of slots, replace the item by a uniform draw from the top-200 scored pool (propensity 0.1 x 1/200 for the pool membership). Log p.
 2. Position-swap on 5% of visits: swap adjacent rows in the same shelf to estimate position effect.
 3. Evaluate new rankers with interleaving (team-draft, Chapelle et al. 2012, N) between two rankers within a single page, because interleaving needs far fewer events than A/B. Power estimate: at a 3% click rate and 20 shown items/visit, 300 visits give 6000 impressions ~ 180 clicks; distinguishing 30% vs 33% preference share needs thousands of clicks, so only large effects (more than 15 points of preference share) are detectable in a month. State this limit before reading any result.

## 7. Falsifiable hypotheses for OpenFeed
H1. Change: log feed_impressions (fields in section 6) with 10% random slots from the top-200. Expected: after 4 weeks there are at least 3,000 impressions with propensities, enough for IPS estimation of CTR. Falsified if fewer than 1,000 impressions with p>0 or if self-normalised IPS CTR of the random slots is not distinguishable from the top-ranked slots (then ranker adds no measurable value). Cost: low (a table insert and one sampler).
H2. Change: Beta-Bernoulli Thompson sampling over channels or topic clusters for 8% of slots, reward = play over 30s. Expected: more distinct channels with a positive event per week than the deterministic ranker. Falsified if distinct positive channels per week do not exceed the control weeks by 2 or more, or if hides/not_interested on those slots exceed 40%. Cost: low-medium (two counters per arm, blending step).
H3. Change: replace hard channel cap with smooth rank-based penalty, 0.8^(prior picks from channel in page). Expected: same channel concentration (Gini) within 5%, higher mean score in top 20. Falsified if top-20 mean score does not improve, or if user hide events per 100 impressions rise. Cost: low.
H4. Change: score subscription freshness by age divided by that channel's median upload gap; discovery items get recency weight 0 to 0.3 of the current. Expected: share of unwatched new subscription uploads within the top 20 rises, and share of items older than 1 year shown falls. Falsified if click-through on subscription uploads from the first week drops, or if the user reports missing new uploads (explicit "missed it" tally). Cost: low (upload-gap computed from the 2k subscription videos).
H5. Change: impression discounting, score x 0.7^k for k prior ignored impressions in 7 days. Expected: overlap between consecutive visits' top 20 falls from the current value to under 50%, with no fall in CTR. Falsified if CTR on the page drops by more than 20% relative, or if repeated items still get clicked at the same rate as fresh ones (so discounting buys nothing). Cost: low once H1 is live.
H6. Change: calibrate topic mix to a prior formed from the 81 subscriptions, shrunk with watches (n/(n+10)). Expected: feed topic distribution KL to the subscription topic distribution falls below 0.3 while hides stay flat. Falsified if hides or not_interested per 100 impressions rise relative to control weeks. Cost: medium (needs topic vectors for all candidates; subscriptions have them).
H7. Change: cross-shelf dedupe plus a single page-level assignment (each video appears once, rows claim greedily by shelf priority). Expected: duplicate rate 0, distinct videos per page up. Falsified if distinct videos per page do not increase, or if a shelf falls below its minimum fill. Cost: low.
H8. Change: weight explicit negatives (hide, not_interested) as channel/topic penalties with decay (e.g. 30-day half-life) instead of per-video. Expected: repeat exposure to the same hidden channel falls to near 0. Falsified if repeat hides of the same channel continue at more than 1 per month, or if blocked channels were later clicked via search (suggests over-penalising). Cost: low-medium.
H9. Change: (interleaving) team-draft interleave current ranker and any new one on the home page. Expected: detects a +15 point preference share in 4 weeks. Falsified if the observed preference share CI over 4 weeks spans 50% for a known-good control (e.g. current ranker versus a deliberately degraded random-60% ranker), meaning the setup cannot detect even a large difference. Cost: medium. Run this A/A and degraded-ranker check before trusting any later result.
